The problem

During an earthquake, shaking at a site is strongest along one horizontal direction (RotD100). Codes treat that direction as random, yet nearby sites often share it. We predict Δθ, the angle difference (0–90°) between two sites, from earthquake, site and distance features: Δθ = f(si, sj, Ek). Three models compete: a distance-only benchmark, ridge regression and a neural network.

What we found

Trend is real

Mean Δθ rises from about 37° (under 10 km) to about 47° (50–100 km), approaching the 45° of random directions, as in the paper.

Models are weak

Test R² is 0.002–0.014 and MAE about 23°. They do not predict single pairs better than a constant.

Why

Only 20 pairs within 5 km, 17 earthquakes, and a 100 km range where directions are mostly unrelated. The network overfits (train 0.084, test 0.014).

Key Points

Δθ is the angle difference between two sites' shaking orientations (0°–90°)

Orientations repeat every 180°, so the difference is folded and cannot exceed 90°. Random orientations would average 45°.

The shaking direction (RotD100) is found by rotating the two components 0–179°

Both horizontal components pass through a damped oscillator (1 s, 5% damping). The response is linear, so each direction is a mix of the two component responses; the direction with the largest peak is kept.

R² near zero: the models do no better than guessing 45°

Always guessing 45° gives an error of about 22.5°, against 22.9–23.0° for our models. We report this as it is. The cause is the data we could get, not the code.

Pairs up to 100 km were used because only 20 pairs were within 5 km

PEER limits waveform downloads to about 200 records per two weeks. With the 198 records we could download, too few pairs were within the paper's 5 km limit.

The neural network overfits the small dataset

About 1,600 training pairs from 17 earthquakes are few. Train R² is 0.084 against 0.014 on test, even with a small network and stronger L2 regularisation.

Train and test pairs may come from the same earthquake (possible leakage)

We split pairs at random, which can overstate scores. The code has a split-by-earthquake option, but 17 earthquakes are too few to use it sensibly.

Feature selection used the validation set, not the test set

The paper describes using the test set; we keep the test set untouched.

Next steps: more waveforms, the 5 km limit, and richer targets

Download more records, split by earthquake, predict the mean and variance of Δθ, and extend to more than two sites.
